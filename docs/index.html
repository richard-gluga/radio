⚡

Why 0.96" Monochrome OLED?

Unlike bulky LCD screens with backlights that leak light, monochrome OLED pixels emit their own light. When a pixel is black, it consumes 0 mA. The entire display draws barely 15mA—saving battery while providing infinite contrast and wide viewing angles.

🔌

Only 4 Jumper Wires (I2C)

It communicates over the ESP32's hardware I2C bus: VCC to 3.3V, GND to Ground, SCL to GPIO 22, and SDA to GPIO 21. No soldering—simply push female Dupont wires onto the header pins!

🛠️

No More Guessing Blind

Without a screen, if Wi-Fi fails or someone typos the password, an audio device sits in complete silence. This display gives crystal-clear feedback in plain English so you know immediately whether to check your router, password, or internet connection.

⚠️

Hardware Pin Allocation Rule

The ESP32 uses GPIO 22 for I2C Clock (SCL). We moved the MAX98357A amplifier's DIN audio line to GPIO 27 so audio and display never collide or glitch!

Acoustics 101

The Secret to Great Sound: "The Baffle Effect"

When you first wire up your 3W speaker on your table, you might think: "Wait, this sounds thin and tinny!"

This is called acoustic phase cancellation. When a speaker cone pushes forward, it creates high air pressure in front, but low pressure behind. With a bare speaker, the sound from the back instantly cancels out the sound from the front!

The moment you mount the speaker inside a sealed wooden box with a cutout hole, the backwards wave is trapped inside. The sound instantly transforms into deep, rich, warm audio.

Pediatric Hearing Safety

Built-In Child-Safe Volume Guard: Never Too Loud

The MAX98357A I2S amplifier outputs up to 3.2 Watts into a 4Ω speaker. In a compact wooden nursery cabinet, uncapped full blast can exceed 92–95 dBA at 1 meter! According to pediatric audiologists and WHO hearing safety guidelines, young children's ear canals amplify high frequencies, so sustained exposure above 80–85 dBA can lead to hearing damage.

🚫

The Danger of Uncapped Knobs

Toddlers love twisting dials and knobs to their physical limits. If a child turns the knob 100% clockwise, an uncapped radio will blast at deafening nursery volumes.

🔒

Software Ear Guard (No Soldering)

We hardcode #define MAX_VOLUME_LIMIT 14 into the ESP32 code. This scales the knob's entire physical rotation smoothly up to a safe ~75 dBA ceiling.

📺

Live OLED Confirmation

The 0.96" OLED screen displays SAFE: 65% and draws a safety ceiling marker on the volume overlay, giving parents instant visual peace of mind.

Follow Along

Step-by-Step Novice Build Walkthrough

Designed for someone who has never touched a microcontroller before. We test everything safely on your desk before putting anything into a box.

Cabinet Interior

How clean it looks when mounted

The speaker is mounted over the round grille, components are secured with hot-glue or small standoffs, and wires are neatly routed.

01
Desktop Bench Test

Test the Audio on Your Desk First (No Box Yet!)

Always test your electronics in the open before mounting them. Plug the ESP32 into your laptop using a standard USB-C data cable (make sure it's a data cable, not a charge-only cable).

  • Use your female-to-female Dupont jumper wires to connect the 5 pins of the MAX98357A to the ESP32 as shown in the pinout table.
  • Connect the two speaker wires into the amplifier's screw terminals or solder pads.
  • Keep the battery disconnected for now — the laptop USB-C cable will power the ESP32 and amplifier during testing!
02
Software Setup

Flash the ESP32 (Takes 5 Minutes in Arduino IDE)

Don't worry if you've never coded in your life. You only need to copy and paste.

1. Download Arduino IDE: Download the free IDE from arduino.cc (Windows/Mac/Linux).
2. Add ESP32 Boards: In Arduino IDE, go to Settings > Additional Board Manager URLs and paste: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/espressif/arduino-esp32/gh-pages/package_esp32_index.json. Then go to Boards Manager, search "esp32", and click Install.
3. Install the Libraries: Go to Tools > Manage Libraries..., search for and install ESP32-audioI2S (by schreibfaul1) and Adafruit SSD1306 (by Adafruit, which automatically includes Adafruit GFX).
4. Flash the Code: Use our code generator below to generate your sketch, select your ESP32 board and COM port in the menu, and hit the Upload arrow!
🎉 The Magic Moment: Within 5 seconds of uploading, your OLED screen will light up, display "Connecting to Wi-Fi", then switch to "ABC Kids listen" with live bouncing audio equalizer bars as music flows from the speaker!
03
Battery & Power

Hook Up the USB-C Rechargeable Battery

Once your desktop bench test works, it's time to make it truly wireless:

  • Connect Battery: Pop your 18650 cell into the battery holder. Wire the holder's Red (+) lead to the TP4056 board's B+ pad and the Black (-) lead to B-.
  • Wire the Power Switch: Wire the TP4056 OUT+ through your toggle switch, then into the ESP32 VIN (and amplifier VIN).
  • Connect Ground: Connect TP4056 OUT- to the ESP32 GND.
  • Test USB-C Charging: Plug a phone charger into the TP4056 USB-C port. The red LED on the charger will glow while charging and turn blue when full!
⚠️ Battery Safety: Never reverse the positive and negative battery wires, and never solder with the 18650 cell installed inside the holder. Always remove the battery while wiring!
04
Cabinet & Finishing

Crafting & Mounting Inside Your Wooden Enclosure

This is where your creation transforms into a cherished family heirloom:

  • Speaker Hole: Use a hole saw or spade bit (usually 40mm to 50mm) to drill a circular hole in the front face.
  • OLED Screen Window: Cut a small 28mm x 15mm rectangular window for the 0.96" OLED display.
  • Vintage Fabric Grille: Glue a small square of linen, tweed, or speaker cloth behind the hole before screwing or gluing the speaker in place.
  • Control Holes: Drill a 6mm hole for the toggle switch and a 7mm hole for the volume potentiometer.
  • USB-C Slot: In the back or side of the box, cut a small 12mm x 4mm slot so the TP4056's USB-C port sits flush with the outside. Secure the TP4056 board with high-strength hot-glue or epoxy.
  • Seal the Box: Ensure the back lid seals tightly. Any air leaks around the speaker will reduce the warm acoustic bass!

// Kangaroo Radio — DIY Child-Safe Multi-Station Radio with OLED
// Hardware: ESP32 + MAX98357A I2S DAC Amp + 0.96" Monochrome OLED + 3W Speaker

#include <Arduino.h>
#include <WiFi.h>
#include <Wire.h>
#include <Adafruit_GFX.h>
#include <Adafruit_SSD1306.h>
#include "Audio.h"

// Wi-Fi Credentials
const char* ssid     = "MyHomeWiFi";
const char* password = "SecretPassword123";

// Pinout Definitions
#define I2S_DOUT      27    // DIN on MAX98357A (moved from 22 to free I2C SCL)
#define I2S_BCLK      26    // BCLK on MAX98357A
#define I2S_LRC       25    // LRC (WS) on MAX98357A
#define I2C_SDA       21    // Hardware I2C SDA (OLED)
#define I2C_SCL       22    // Hardware I2C SCL (OLED)
#define BUTTON_PIN    33    // Station Cycle Push Button (to GND)
#define POT_PIN       34    // Volume Potentiometer (ADC1, 3.3V reference)

// Child-Safe Volume Safety Cap (Scales 10k knob smoothly to safe ceiling)
#define MAX_VOLUME_LIMIT 14  // Safe ceiling (~75 dBA)

// 0.96" Monochrome SSD1306 OLED (128x64)
#define SCREEN_WIDTH  128
#define SCREEN_HEIGHT 64
Adafruit_SSD1306 display(SCREEN_WIDTH, SCREEN_HEIGHT, &Wire, -1);
Audio audio;

struct Station {
  const char* name;
  const char* genre;
  const char* url;
};

const Station stations[] = {
  { "ABC Kids listen", "Preschool & Aussie Stories", "https://streaming.abc-cdn.net.au/audio/hls/abcextra.m3u8?source=web" },
  { "Fun Kids UK", "Pop, Stories & Quizzes", "https://listen-funkids.sharp-stream.com/funkids.mp3" },
  { "Fun Kids Junior", "Gentle Nursery Rhymes (0-4)", "http://listen-funkids.sharp-stream.com/funkidsjunior.mp3" },
  { "Rock the Cradle", "Indie Music for Families", "http://rockthecradle.stream.publicradio.org/rockthecradle.mp3" },
  { "Positively Sleep", "Calm Lullabies & Ambient", "https://streaming.positivity.radio/pr/sleepkids/icecast.audio" },
  { "ABC Classic", "Peaceful Classical for Play", "https://streaming.abc-cdn.net.au/audio/hls/classicfmnsw.m3u8?source=web" }
};

const int TOTAL_STATIONS = sizeof(stations) / sizeof(stations[0]);
int currentStation = 0;
int lastVolume = -1;
unsigned long lastButtonPress = 0;

void drawTopBar(const char* tag, int vol) {
  display.fillRect(0, 0, SCREEN_WIDTH, 11, SSD1306_WHITE);
  display.setTextColor(SSD1306_BLACK, SSD1306_WHITE);
  display.setTextSize(1);
  display.setCursor(2, 2); display.print(tag);
  display.setCursor(74, 2); display.print("SAFE:");
  display.print(map(vol, 0, MAX_VOLUME_LIMIT, 0, 100)); display.print("%");
  display.setTextColor(SSD1306_WHITE);
}

void showConnectingScreen(const char* targetSsid, int progress) {
  display.clearDisplay();
  drawTopBar("KANGAROO RADIO", 14);
  display.setCursor(0, 18); display.print("Connecting to Wi-Fi");
  display.setCursor(0, 30); display.print("SSID: "); display.print(targetSsid);
  display.drawRect(4, 45, 120, 10, SSD1306_WHITE);
  display.fillRect(6, 47, map(progress, 0, 35, 10, 116), 6, SSD1306_WHITE);
  display.display();
}

void showErrorScreen(const char* title, const char* line1, const char* line2) {
  display.clearDisplay();
  display.fillRect(0, 0, SCREEN_WIDTH, 13, SSD1306_WHITE);
  display.setTextColor(SSD1306_BLACK, SSD1306_WHITE);
  display.setCursor(14, 3); display.print("[ ! ] ERROR [ ! ]");
  display.setTextColor(SSD1306_WHITE);
  display.setCursor(0, 20); display.print("Reason: "); display.println(title);
  display.setCursor(0, 34); display.println(line1);
  display.setCursor(0, 48); display.println(line2);
  display.display();
}

void showStationScreen(int idx, int vol, int tick = 0) {
  display.clearDisplay();
  drawTopBar("WIFI OK", vol);
  display.setCursor(0, 16); display.print("STATION ["); display.print(idx + 1); display.print("/6]");
  display.setCursor(0, 28); display.print(stations[idx].name);
  display.setCursor(0, 40); display.print(stations[idx].genre);
  int bars[] = {4, 7, 11, 8, 13, 9, 6, 12, 10, 5, 8, 14, 7, 10, 6, 12};
  for (int i = 0; i < 16; i++) {
    int h = bars[(i + tick) % 16];
    display.fillRect(4 + (i * 7), 63 - h, 4, h, SSD1306_WHITE);
  }
  display.display();
}

void switchStation(int index) {
  if (index < 0 || index >= TOTAL_STATIONS) return;
  currentStation = index;
  showStationScreen(currentStation, lastVolume >= 0 ? lastVolume : 14);
  audio.connecttohost(stations[currentStation].url);
}

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  pinMode(BUTTON_PIN, INPUT_PULLUP);

  // Initialize OLED Display (Hardware I2C SDA=21, SCL=22)
  Wire.begin(I2C_SDA, I2C_SCL);
  display.begin(SSD1306_SWITCHCAPVCC, 0x3C);

  // Connect to Wi-Fi with live OLED diagnostic feedback
  WiFi.mode(WIFI_STA);
  WiFi.begin(ssid, password);
  int attempts = 0;
  while (WiFi.status() != WL_CONNECTED && attempts < 35) {
    delay(350);
    showConnectingScreen(ssid, attempts);
    attempts++;
  }

  if (WiFi.status() != WL_CONNECTED) {
    if (WiFi.status() == WL_NO_SSID_AVAIL) {
      showErrorScreen("SSID NOT FOUND", "Wifi not found", "Check router 2.4GHz");
    } else {
      showErrorScreen("WRONG PASSWORD", "Auth failed on:", ssid);
    }
    while (WiFi.status() != WL_CONNECTED) { delay(1000); }
  }

  // Audio setup
  audio.setPinout(I2S_BCLK, I2S_LRC, I2S_DOUT);
  audio.setVolume(14);
  switchStation(currentStation);
}

void loop() {
  audio.loop();

  // Push button station cycling
  if (digitalRead(BUTTON_PIN) == LOW) {
    if (millis() - lastButtonPress > 450) {
      lastButtonPress = millis();
      switchStation((currentStation + 1) % TOTAL_STATIONS);
    }
  }

  // Volume potentiometer check with Child-Safe Cap
  static unsigned long lastPot = 0;
  if (millis() - lastPot > 200) {
    lastPot = millis();
    int newVol = map(analogRead(POT_PIN), 0, 4095, 0, MAX_VOLUME_LIMIT);
    if (abs(newVol - lastVolume) > 0) {
      lastVolume = newVol;
      audio.setVolume(newVol);
      showStationScreen(currentStation, lastVolume);
    }
  }
}
Avoid the Traps

Common Gotchas, Downsides & Fixes

Everything that could possibly trip up a novice builder, with instant fixes.

📶

Gotcha #1: 2.4 GHz vs 5 GHz Wi-Fi

The ESP32 microcontroller only supports 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. If your modern home router has a single combined Wi-Fi name for both 2.4GHz and 5GHz, the ESP32 can occasionally struggle to connect on first try.

Fix: Log in to your home router and enable a dedicated 2.4GHz network name (or enable your router's "Guest Network" on 2.4 GHz).
🌏

Gotcha #2: Australian Geoblocking

Due to licensing for children's music and stories (like Bluey and Play School), the ABC strictly restricts its digital streams to Australian IP addresses.

Fix: This radio will work seamlessly in Sydney and anywhere in Australia. If taking it overseas on holiday, you'd need an in-home VPN or local proxy.
🚗

Gotcha #3: Taking It in the Car or Camping

Because it uses Wi-Fi, what happens when you take the kids to grandma's or in the car?

Fix: In the code, you can add your phone's mobile hotspot name and password as a secondary network! When away from home, switch on your phone hotspot and the radio connects automatically.
🔌

Gotcha #4: Low-Volume Buzzing or Wi-Fi Whine

When the ESP32 transmits Wi-Fi packets, tiny power dips can cause a faint squeaking or buzzing in the speaker if power rails are shared carelessly.

Fix: Add a cheap 220µF electrolytic capacitor directly across the VIN and GND pins of the MAX98357A amplifier. It smooths power fluctuations completely!
🔋

Gotcha #5: Over-Discharging Lithium Batteries

If a standard 18650 cell drops below 2.5V, it can permanently lose capacity or become dangerous to recharge.

Fix: Only use the TP4056 board version that includes the DW01A protection chip (distinguished by 4 solder pads: B+, B-, OUT+, OUT-), which cuts off power automatically at 2.9V.
🛠️

Gotcha #6: Solder-Free Dupont Wires Coming Loose

If you build the entire project with solderless push-on jumper wires, tossing the radio in a backpack could shake a wire loose.

Fix: Once your bench test is 100% verified, apply a small dab of hot-glue over the plastic pin headers. It holds firmly but can easily be peeled off later if you ever want to reconfigure it!
Optional Alternative

What If You Genuinely Want an Over-The-Air FM Radio?

If you want to build a radio for traditional Sydney FM stations (like Triple J 105.7 FM, ABC Classic 92.9 FM, or 2DayFM 104.1 FM), or you want to set up a small in-home FM transmitter:

The FM Parts Swap:

  • Swap the MAX98357A for an RDA5807M or TEA5767 FM receiver breakout module (~$3.50 AUD).
  • Add a PAM8403 (3W stereo) or PAM8302 (mono) analog amplifier board.
  • Attach a 75cm thin wire antenna to the ANT pin on the FM module.

Hardcoded Auto-Tune Frequency (Arduino):

#include <Wire.h>
#include <radio.h>
#include <RDA5807M.h>

RDA5807M radio;

void setup() {
  radio.init();
  radio.setBandFrequency(RADIO_BAND_FM, 10570); // 105.7 Triple J Sydney
  radio.setVolume(12);
}
Locks straight onto 105.7 MHz whenever power is turned on!
